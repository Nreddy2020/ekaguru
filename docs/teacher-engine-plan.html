Teacher Engine

A book goes in. Every page comes out prepared. On the board, Guru opens the lesson, teaches each idea in depth, stops on every new word, asks whether the child is following, takes doubts, and carries on. This page is the architecture and the ordered tasks to get there.

First, the daily limit

Free chat sessions are not the way out. Here is what is.

What is actually limited. The Gemini free tier allows 20 requests per model per day. That blocked preparation: writing a lesson costs three or four calls. Teaching is different. Once a lesson is prepared it is cached and every learner on that page uses it for nothing; a child's own turns are one small call each. The quota is a one-time cost per page, shared by all users, never a per-user cost.

Why the app cannot drive ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude chat windows. Automating the consumer sites with a bot breaks their terms of use, and a banned account stops the app for every learner at once. Captchas, session limits and interface changes make every week an outage. Children's answers and uploaded textbooks would pass through a personal chat account outside any data agreement. And the consumer plans have hourly limits of their own. It is not a foundation a product can stand on, so it is not in this plan.

Definition of done

What "teach like a real teacher" means, on page 3 of EVS Class 5

  1. Open the lesson. "Today's lesson is I am Growing Up. By the end you will be able to say what living things do that non-living things do not, and you will know three new words: seed, sprout, grow." Objectives come from the page's learning outcomes when it has them, from the prepared topic map when it does not.
  2. Explain each idea in depth, on the board. Heading, keywords and a diagram appear while Guru speaks; the concept follows the Experience to Symbol order the blueprint already enforces, with a real-life example from the child's world.
  3. Stop on every new word. When "baby plant" appears, Guru does not move on: how a baby plant is born (a seed with food inside, water, warmth, root first, then shoot), how it grows (light, water, soil, weeks to months), with a staged drawing. These term ladders are prepared in advance for every key term.
  4. Check understanding after each idea. "Are you with me? Tell me in your own words what a seed needs." The child answers by voice or typing. Guru reads the answer as understood, partly, confused, a question, off topic, or silence, and responds to that.
  5. Re-explain differently when the child is lost. Not the same sentence louder: a second explanation with another analogy or a hands-on picture, then a third. Each concept carries a ladder of three prepared variants before a live model call is needed.
  6. Take doubts at any moment. "Guru, what is a root?" interrupts the explanation. Guru answers from the page and the topic map, says plainly when the answer goes beyond this page, and returns to where the lesson was, with the board still marking the spot.
  7. Anticipate doubts. Every page has a prepared doubt bank, the questions children of this age ask about this content, so common doubts are answered instantly and consistently.
  8. Practise, then summarise. Worked example, guided practice, independent practice, summary in the page's words, and a teach-back, as the blueprint has today.
  9. Remember the child. What was understood, what confused them and what they asked feeds the next page. "Guru remembers" exists; it must now carry check-in outcomes and doubts.
  10. Sound like a teacher. One consistent persona, addressing the child by name, warm, never "as an AI"; every sentence spoken by the orb with captions under it.

A lesson that does all ten for any page of an uploaded book, with no human preparing anything, is the definition of done for this stage.

Architecture

Five parts. Green marks what already runs.

A · Model Fabric

Provider registry with roles: vision, prepare, converse, grade. Gemini, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (local models, free tiers), Claude. Per-provider daily quota ledger and failover.

exists: Gemini live, budgets, quota detection

B · Book Preparation

On upload: read every page, build a topic map per page (terms, ladders, doubt bank, objectives), then lessons at five depths under blueprint v4, prioritised around where learners are.

exists: queued OCR, planner v3, lesson jobs

C · Teacher Dialogue

Beats with a stack instead of a straight line. Check-in loop with a six-label classifier and policies, grounded doubt answering, interrupts and resume, persona rules, transcript ledger.

exists: session runtime, graded asks, page questions

D · Board and Voice

Board plans written as spoken, staged diagrams, circle and underline on re-explain, "we are here" marker, listening mode with barge-in, doubt panel, preparation status.

exists: board, scenes, orb, speech in and out

E · Understanding, memory, quality

Check-in outcomes and doubts per learner and concept feed the next page and the parent view. Persona replay evaluations (confused, curious, silent, off-topic learners) run without humans; educators spot-check topic maps; latency and cost are measured. Exists: mastery gate, spaced reviews, educator corpus.

New data and endpoints

Tables GuruProviderUsage, GuruBookPreparation, GuruPageTopicMap, GuruDialogueTurn; lesson artifacts gain blueprint v4 fields (orientation, term, check-in, ladder, board plan). Endpoints to prepare a book and read its status, to read a page's topic map, session events for check-in, doubt, resume and interrupt, and an admin view of provider health and quota.

The 40 percent

When this stage is complete

A book uploaded by a parent is prepared end to end with no human step. Any of its pages opens with the orientation. The ideas are taught on the board with new words explained in depth. The teacher checks understanding after each idea and reacts to the answer. Doubts are taken and answered at any moment and the lesson resumes where it was. All of it works by voice, on more than one provider, at a cost that does not grow with the number of learners. The evidence for each claim is the acceptance line of the task that delivers it, recorded in the repository's evaluation notes.
